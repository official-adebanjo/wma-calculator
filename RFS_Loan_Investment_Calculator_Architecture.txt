ROSABON FINANCIAL SERVICES (RFS) LOAN AND INVESTMENT CALCULATOR WEB APP
SOLUTION ARCHITECTURE AND DEVELOPMENT SPECIFICATION

Document status: Draft for engineering planning Version: 1.0 Date: 27
October 2026 Purpose: Build a QA-focused web application that calculates
estimates and detailed breakdowns for RFS loan and investment products,
using approved product rules and PRD-backed configuration.

====================================================================== 1.
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
======================================================================

Build a web application that allows a tester or authorized user to
select an RFS loan or investment product, enter the relevant
principal/funding amount and tenor, choose product-specific options, and
receive a transparent calculation breakdown.

The app is primarily a QA calculation/reference tool. It should
support: - Estimates and detailed breakdowns for loan and investment
products. - Product-specific validation of amount, tenor, rate, and
required options. - A calculation explanation showing the formula and
values used. - Scenario comparison, calculation history, and exportable
results. - Versioned product rules and rate tables so expected results
can be reproduced. - A clear distinction between confirmed PRD rules,
illustrative examples, configurable values, and unresolved rules.

The calculator must not silently invent rates or formulas. If an
approved rate or calculation rule is unavailable, it must either show a
validation-only result or mark the numerical result as BLOCKED /
CONFIGURATION REQUIRED. This is especially important for REAP and RWIN,
whose approved calculation tables/rules are not complete in the reviewed
materials.

This is a calculation and QA support tool. It must not disburse loans,
accept investment funds, create a live investment, debit wallets, or act
as the source of truth for customer balances. The Core Banking
Application (CBA/CBS) and approved RFS systems remain authoritative for
live transactions and ledger values.

======================================================================
2. OBJECTIVES
======================================================================

2.1 Primary objectives - Calculate estimates consistently for each
supported loan and investment product. - Display principal, rate, tenor,
interest/return, tax or deductions, total payable/payout, and
installment schedule where applicable. - Let QA compare calculator
output against the app, API, Admin Portal, ledger, and approved test
cases. - Make each result reproducible using the exact product
configuration and rule version used. - Prevent unsupported product
combinations from returning misleading figures. - Support future
products by adding product configuration and a calculation strategy
rather than rewriting the entire application.

2.2 Non-goals - No live loan application, underwriting, approval,
signing, disbursement, or repayment collection. - No investment booking,
funding, withdrawal, rollover, or ledger mutation. - No direct
integration to BVN/NIN, credit bureaus, bank accounts, payment
providers, or wallets in the MVP. - No claim that an estimate is an
approved customer offer unless explicitly sourced from an approved
configuration. - No replacement of the RFS CBA/CBS, production loan
engine, or production investment calculation service.

3. SOURCE DOCUMENTS AND RULE STATUS
======================================================================

The design is based on the reviewed RFS materials: - LOAN PRODUCT
SPECIFICATIONS.docx - RFS Unified App Asset Creation PRD.docx - Rosabon
Unified App — Investment Customer Journeys.docx - Rosabon QA Calculation
Matrix.xlsx

Implementation must preserve the following source-status distinctions.

3.1 Confirmed or specified calculation patterns

NANO LOAN - Principal range: NGN 5,000 to NGN 50,000, configurable. -
Tenor: 1 to 30 days. - Example daily rate: 0.30% per day. - Interest =
Principal x daily rate x number of days. - Total repayable = Principal +
interest. - Example: NGN 20,000 for 7 days at 0.30% per day gives NGN
420 interest and NGN 20,420 total repayable.

PAYDAY LOAN - Maximum amount: up to NGN 500,000, subject to
configuration and salary evaluation. - Tenor: 7 to 30 days. - Example
daily rate: 0.30% per day. - Interest = Principal x daily rate x number
of days. - Total repayable = Principal + interest. - Early settlement
interest is recalculated/capped using actual days used. - Example: NGN
200,000 for 15 days at 0.30% per day gives NGN 9,000 interest and NGN
209,000 total repayable.

PERSONAL LOAN — PRIVATE SECTOR - Maximum amount: up to NGN 30,000,000,
configurable. - Tenor: 3 to 24 months, configurable. - Interest model is
configurable: monthly flat or reducing balance. - Private-sector loan
specification states early repayment is allowed without penalty and
future unearned interest is waived. - Illustrative flat example in
source: NGN 6,000,000 over 6 months at 2% monthly; NGN 720,000 total
interest; NGN 6,720,000 total repayable; NGN 1,120,000 equal monthly due
when principal is repaid equally. - Illustrative reducing-balance
example in source: NGN 6,000,000 over 6 months at 2% monthly; total
interest NGN 420,000; total repayable NGN 6,420,000; installment amounts
reduce from NGN 1,120,000 to NGN 1,020,000.

RTN — ROSABON TREASURY NOTE - Principal bands: Band A: NGN 50,000 to NGN
999,999.99. Band B: NGN 1,000,000 to NGN 9,999,999.99. Band C: NGN
10,000,000 and above. - Allowed tenors: 60, 90, 120, 180, and 365
days. - Rates vary by band, tenor, and payout option (upfront or at
maturity). Rates are annual percentages and must be loaded from the
current approved RTN Rate Guide. Do not hardcode the table as
permanently authoritative. - At-maturity formula: gross interest =
principal x annual rate x actual days / 365. - Withholding tax (WHT) =
10% of gross interest. - Net interest = gross interest - WHT. - Maturity
payout = principal + net interest. - Example in source: NGN 1,000,000,
90 days, Band B maturity rate 18.75%: gross interest NGN 46,232.88; WHT
NGN 4,623.29; net interest NGN 41,609.59; payout NGN 1,041,609.59. -
Upfront option uses a discounted booked principal calculation. The
reviewed journey example uses divisor = 1 + ((1 - WHT rate) x annual
rate x tenor / 365), booked principal = funded amount / divisor, and
upfront interest = funded amount - booked principal. The approved rule,
rounding, and presentation must be confirmed with
Product/Treasury/Finance before being treated as authoritative. - Early
withdrawal, top-ups, partial withdrawals, penalty-band boundaries,
upfront-interest claw-back, maturity default, and weekend/holiday
handling contain proposed or open rules. These must be configurable and
marked as unresolved until approved.

REAP AND RWIN - The reviewed materials do not provide complete approved
rate tables and calculation rules for reliable numerical estimates. -
Journey documents identify inconsistent/incorrect projections and
missing-rate behavior. - RWIN minimum is described as NGN 50,000 in the
journey material; another earlier app note suggests a conflicting
minimum. Use the approved current product configuration as source of
truth and resolve the conflict before numerical acceptance testing. -
REAP has previously shown an amount/tenor combination without an
available rate while still displaying a projection. The calculator must
never do this. - Until rates and formulas are approved, REAP/RWIN may
support input validation and configuration-status display, but numeric
return calculation must remain disabled or clearly marked BLOCKED.

3.2 Rules that must not be guessed Before enabling a production-quality
calculation for any product, obtain approved decisions for: - Rate, rate
unit, rate effective date, and source/version. - Whether rate is daily,
monthly, or annual. - Simple vs compound interest. - Day-count
convention (365, 360, actual/actual, or another approved basis). -
Rounding policy: component-level, per-day, per-installment, or
final-only. - Minimum/maximum principal and tenor. - Rate band
boundaries and inclusivity. - Due-date, maturity-date, weekends, and
public holidays. - Early settlement, partial repayment, penalty,
claw-back, rebate, and fees. - Product-specific eligibility and whether
eligibility affects calculator limits. - How configuration changes
affect existing quotes and saved scenarios.

4. USERS AND ACCESS
======================================================================

MVP roles: 1. QA Tester - Select products and enter test inputs. - Run
calculations and compare scenarios. - Save and export calculation
results. - View calculation formula, configuration version, and
warnings.

2.  Product/Finance Reviewer
    -   Review calculation outputs and unresolved rule questions.
    -   In MVP, product configuration may be maintained through a
        controlled configuration file or seeded database.
    -   If an admin screen is later added, rate/rule changes must be
        authenticated, audited, versioned, and approved.
3.  Administrator
    -   Manage users, product configuration, rate tables, rule versions,
        and audit logs.
    -   Cannot silently alter a historical calculation; changes create a
        new configuration version.

The app should be private to the organization or protected behind
authentication if deployed beyond a local developer environment. Do not
expose internal product rates or test data publicly without
authorization.

5. RECOMMENDED TECHNOLOGY STACK
======================================================================

Frontend: - Next.js App Router - TypeScript - Tailwind CSS - A reusable
component system such as shadcn/ui, if desired - React Hook Form plus
Zod for input validation - Recharts only if charts materially help

Backend: - Node.js with TypeScript - Next.js Route Handlers for a small
single-deployment MVP, or a separate Node.js API service if calculation
logic will be reused by other applications - Zod for API
request/response validation - A dedicated calculation domain/service
layer that has no dependency on UI components

Persistence: - PostgreSQL for users, saved scenarios, configuration
versions, and audit records - Prisma ORM or an equivalent typed
data-access layer - For a local-only prototype, configuration can begin
in version-controlled JSON files, but historical runs should record a
snapshot/version identifier.

Testing: - Vitest or Jest for unit tests - Playwright for end-to-end
browser tests - Approved examples in the QA Calculation Matrix as
golden/reference test cases - Optional CI pipeline to run unit,
integration, and UI tests on every change

Deployment: - Internal test/staging environment first. - Use environment
variables for secrets and deployment configuration. - No secrets, real
customer data, or production credentials in source control.

Architecture should remain modular so that the UI, calculation engine,
configuration store, and persistence layer can be tested independently.

7. APPLICATION MODULES
======================================================================

7.1 Product Catalogue - Separate Loan Calculators and Investment
Calculators. - Show product name, supported calculation status,
minimum/maximum amount, tenor options, interest model, and any
configuration warning. - Do not show a product as numerically calculable
if required rates or formulas are missing. - Products can be marked:
ACTIVE: approved rules and rates exist. VALIDATION_ONLY: input limits
can be checked, but no approved numerical formula exists. BLOCKED:
required rules/configuration are missing or expired. DEPRECATED:
retained for historical scenarios but not available for new runs.

7.2 Dynamic Input Form - Render fields from product configuration. -
Common fields: principal/funding amount, tenor, rate option where
relevant, start date/value date if required, calculation date, and
product-specific options. - Validate minimum, maximum, allowed tenor,
required options, numeric precision, and rate availability. - Display
field-level errors and a clear explanation. - Recalculate only when all
required fields are valid. - Clearly label any manually overridden rate
as a QA override and distinguish it from approved configuration.

7.3 Calculation Results Every successful calculation should include: -
Product and product code. - Principal/funding amount. - Selected tenor
and unit. - Rate and rate unit (e.g. 0.30% per day or 18.75% p.a.). -
Rate source and configuration version/effective date. - Gross
interest/return. - Tax, withholding tax, fees, penalties, or other
deductions, where applicable. - Net interest/return. - Total repayment
or maturity payout. - Installment schedule for installment-based
loans. - Early-settlement or early-withdrawal estimate when the rules
are approved. - Formula and calculation steps. - Warnings, assumptions,
and any rules not yet approved. - A result status: CALCULATED,
VALIDATION_ONLY, BLOCKED, or ERROR.

7.4 Scenario Comparison - Compare two or more valid scenarios for the
same product or supported products. - Show input differences and output
differences side-by-side. - For investments, compare tenor and payout
option where supported. - For loans, compare interest model, tenor,
rate, total interest, installment, and total repayment. - Do not compare
outputs produced under incompatible or unapproved rule versions without
a warning.

7.5 Scenario History - Save named scenarios. - Store the input values,
output breakdown, formula version, rate/configuration version,
calculation timestamp, user, and warnings. - Historical results must be
immutable snapshots. Recalculating after a rate update creates a new
result rather than silently changing an old result. - Allow CSV export
in MVP; PDF export can be added later.

7.6 Configuration Management For MVP, configuration may be seeded from
checked-in JSON and reviewed by Engineering/Product. A future admin
interface should support: - Product activation status. - Principal
bounds and allowed tenors. - Rate tables and rate effective dates. -
Interest model and calculation method. - Tax/withholding settings. -
Rounding rules. - Early repayment/withdrawal rules. - Versioning, change
reason, author, approver, and audit trail. - Draft, pending approval,
active, and retired states. - Validation that prevents activation when
required fields are missing.

8. PRODUCT CALCULATION SPECIFICATIONS
======================================================================

8.1 Shared calculation rules - Use decimal arithmetic or integer minor
units (kobo) for monetary values. Do not use binary floating-point
arithmetic for money calculations. - Preserve full precision during
intermediate steps according to the approved rounding policy. - Format
NGN values consistently with two decimal places for display. - Return
both raw numeric values and formatted display values if needed. - Never
silently substitute a default rate if a rate is missing. - Always return
the exact formula/version and input values used. - The same domain
calculation service should power the UI, API response, saved scenario,
and exported result.

8.2 Nano Loan Inputs: - Principal amount. - Tenor in days. - Daily rate
from active product configuration.

Validations: - Principal within configured minimum and maximum; source
example: NGN 5,000–NGN 50,000. - Tenor between 1 and 30 days. - Rate
exists and is active.

Calculation: Interest = Principal x Daily Rate x Tenor Days Total
Repayable = Principal + Interest

Output: - Principal. - Daily rate. - Number of days. - Interest. - Total
repayable. - If the PRD does not define a multi-installment schedule,
show one amount due at maturity rather than inventing installments.

8.3 Payday Loan Inputs: - Principal. - Tenor in days. - Daily rate from
active configuration. - Actual settlement day for early settlement
preview, if enabled.

Validations: - Tenor 7–30 days. - Principal does not exceed the
configured product cap; maximum in source is up to NGN 500,000, subject
to salary evaluation. - Rate exists.

Calculation at scheduled maturity: Interest = Principal x Daily Rate x
Selected Tenor Days Total Repayable = Principal + Interest

Early settlement preview: - Calculate interest using actual days used,
capped at the scheduled tenor. - Formula and exact day-count behavior
must match the approved implementation. - Do not add an early settlement
fee unless Product/Finance approves it.

8.4 Personal Loan — Private Sector Inputs: - Principal. - Tenor in
months. - Monthly rate. - Interest model: FLAT or REDUCING_BALANCE. -
Optional payoff month/date for early settlement preview, only when
approved rules are configured.

Validations: - Principal above zero and not greater than configured
maximum; source maximum up to NGN 30,000,000. - Tenor 3–24 months. -
Interest model is explicitly selected/configured. - Rate unit must be
MONTHLY if applying the source examples. Never interpret a monthly rate
as an annual rate or vice versa.

Flat model, consistent with source worked example: - Equal principal
component = Principal / Number of Months. - Interest per month =
Original Principal x Monthly Rate. - Monthly installment = Equal
principal component + monthly interest. - Total interest = Monthly
interest x Number of Months. - Total repayable = Principal + Total
interest. - If the approved product implementation defines another flat
repayment convention, configure it explicitly rather than silently
changing this method.

Reducing-balance model, consistent with source worked example: - Equal
principal component = Principal / Number of Months. - At each period,
interest = Opening Principal Balance x Monthly Rate. - Installment =
Principal Component + Period Interest. - Closing Balance = Opening
Balance - Principal Component. - Total interest = sum of period
interest. - Total repayable = Principal + Total interest. - The approved
PRD example uses equal principal repayments and declining interest; do
not substitute a standard amortizing-payment formula unless the business
specification explicitly requires it.

Early repayment: - Private-sector specification states early repayment
is allowed without penalty and future unearned interest is waived. - The
precise payoff quote calculation, accrued-interest convention, and
treatment of current-period interest must be approved and implemented
before the feature is enabled. - Broader PRD language describes
product-specific penalty/rebate logic in some flows. Product-specific
policy takes precedence only after Product/Finance confirms the
authoritative rule.

8.5 Public-Sector Loan and other loan products - The broader Asset
Creation PRD describes additional loan/lease products, but the reviewed
loan specification does not provide complete calculation rules for every
product. - Add a product module only after Product/Finance supplies
approved limits, tenor, rate unit, interest model, repayment convention,
and early-settlement rules. - Do not reuse private-sector rates or
formulas for public-sector, group, SME, collateralized, study, BNPL, or
lease products by assumption. - For unconfigured products, show
VALIDATION_ONLY or BLOCKED status.

8.6 RTN — At-Maturity Option Inputs: - Funding principal. - Tenor
selected from 60, 90, 120, 180, 365 days. - Active RTN Rate Guide
version. - Value/start date if maturity date is to be displayed.

Band selection: - Band A: NGN 50,000–NGN 999,999.99. - Band B: NGN
1,000,000–NGN 9,999,999.99. - Band C: NGN 10,000,000 and above. -
Confirm boundary treatment and current active rate guide with
Product/Treasury.

Calculation: Gross Interest = Principal x Annual Rate x Actual Days /
365 WHT = Gross Interest x 10% Net Interest = Gross Interest - WHT
Maturity Payout = Principal + Net Interest

Display: - Principal. - Band. - Rate and rate guide version. - Tenor and
estimated maturity date. - Gross interest. - WHT. - Net interest. -
Maturity payout. - Formula and calculation assumptions.

8.7 RTN — Upfront Option - Implement only after the current approved
formula, rounding, tax treatment, and booking display have been signed
off. - The reviewed journey uses a discounted booked-principal formula:
Divisor = 1 + ((1 - WHT Rate) x Annual Rate x Tenor Days / 365) Booked
Principal = Funded Amount / Divisor Upfront Interest = Funded Amount -
Booked Principal - This formula is documented in the reviewed journey,
but the configuration and rounding behavior need approval before being
treated as final. - Display funded amount, rate, tenor, booked
principal, upfront interest, and any applicable tax treatment
separately. - Do not imply that the upfront interest amount is the same
as the maturity option’s interest.

8.8 RTN — Early Withdrawal, Top-Up, Partial Withdrawal - Design the
calculation engine to support these as separate strategies, but keep
each disabled until the open rules are approved. - Open questions
include penalty-band gaps/boundaries, upfront interest claw-back, top-up
rate treatment, partial withdrawal re-rating, maturity default, and
weekend/holiday handling. - The UI may expose a clearly marked preview
only if stakeholders approve a provisional test mode. Provisional
outputs must never be labelled confirmed. - Include the elapsed days,
elapsed-tenor percentage, penalty rate, gross interest, penalty, taxable
amount, WHT, net interest, and payout where applicable.

8.9 REAP - Build the UI and calculation interface, but set product
status to VALIDATION_ONLY or BLOCKED until the approved rate table,
calculation formula, plan type, contribution rules, tenor/target-date
behavior, and maturity rules are provided. - Do not hardcode 30% p.a. or
any other assumed rate. - If the selected amount/tenor has no approved
rate, display a message such as: “No approved rate is available for this
combination. Calculation is unavailable.” - Once approved rules are
supplied, add the strategy behind the same shared calculation interface
and create golden test cases before activation.

8.10 RWIN - Build the UI and calculation interface, but set status to
VALIDATION_ONLY or BLOCKED until the approved rate table, cycle length,
return formula, maturity rules, and any prize/reward rules are
confirmed. - Use the currently approved configured minimum amount;
resolve conflicting source notes before activation. - Do not reuse RTN
rates or formulas. - No fallback/default projection is allowed when a
rate is missing. - Once approved, test projections for sanity and parity
between quote, setup summary, booking, placement, and certificate.

10. API DESIGN
======================================================================

Example endpoints for a Next.js Route Handler or separate API:

GET /api/products - Returns product catalogue and calculation status.

GET /api/products/{productCode} - Returns product metadata, valid input
constraints, allowed tenors/options, and active configuration status. -
Avoid exposing restricted configuration to unauthorized users.

POST /api/calculations Request example: { “productCode”: “NANO”,
“inputs”: { “principal”: “20000.00”, “tenor”: 7, “tenorUnit”: “DAY” } }

Response shape: { “status”: “CALCULATED”, “productCode”: “NANO”,
“configVersion”: “NANO-1.0”, “inputs”: {}, “summary”: { “principal”:
“20000.00”, “grossInterest”: “420.00”, “tax”: “0.00”, “netInterest”:
“420.00”, “totalRepayable”: “20420.00” }, “lineItems”: [], “formula”:
“interest = principal x dailyRate x tenorDays”, “assumptions”: [],
“warnings”: [] }

The JSON above is illustrative. Final API schema should use validated
decimal strings or a decimal-safe representation for monetary values.

POST /api/scenarios - Saves a calculation snapshot and user-provided
scenario name.

GET /api/scenarios - Returns scenarios available to the authenticated
user.

GET /api/scenarios/{id} - Returns the immutable saved inputs, outputs,
configuration version, formula, and warnings.

POST /api/scenarios/compare - Accepts scenario IDs or calculation inputs
and returns a normalized comparison.

GET /api/config/versions/{productCode} - Admin/reviewer-only endpoint to
inspect versions and status.

POST /api/config/versions - Admin-only draft creation endpoint;
activation requires approval workflow if an admin interface is
implemented.

Error response conventions: - 400: malformed input or invalid fields. -
401/403: authentication/authorization failure. - 404: unknown
product/scenario. - 409: no active configuration, stale configuration,
or conflicting version state. - 422: valid request structure but
unsupported product combination/rule. - 500: unexpected system error;
never return a plausible-looking zero calculation.

======================================================================
12. VALIDATION AND ERROR HANDLING
======================================================================

Input validation: - Reject negative values, zero principal where not
allowed, non-numeric values, unsupported decimal precision, and missing
fields. - Validate tenor against product rules. - Validate selected rate
option and tenor combination against active rate table. - Handle band
boundaries precisely. - Detect stale/missing configuration. - If product
is BLOCKED, do not calculate a numeric output. - If product is
VALIDATION_ONLY, perform only allowed validation and state that
numerical calculation is unavailable. - Never coerce an invalid value to
zero. - Do not allow NaN, Infinity, or unbounded numeric values to reach
the output.

Calculation failures: - Return a stable error code and human-readable
message. - Log technical details server-side without exposing secrets or
internal stack traces. - Preserve the user’s inputs so they can correct
the error. - Record the config version attempted where appropriate.

Configuration failures: - If no active rate exists, return
CONFIGURATION_UNAVAILABLE. - If a rate has expired, do not silently use
it; show the expiry or refresh requirement. - If multiple active rates
overlap, block calculation and log a configuration integrity error.

======================================================================
13. SECURITY, PRIVACY, AND AUDIT
======================================================================

-   Use authentication for shared/staging deployments.
-   Enforce role-based access to configuration and audit pages.
-   Validate all API requests on the server, even if the frontend
    validates them.
-   Use HTTPS in deployed environments.
-   Store credentials and secrets in environment variables or a managed
    secret store.
-   Do not use real customer BVN, NIN, bank details, salary data, or
    account identifiers in sample calculations.
-   Use synthetic test data only.
-   Apply secure session and CSRF protections appropriate to the chosen
    authentication model.
-   Rate-limit public or shared endpoints where appropriate.
-   Audit configuration changes and approvals.
-   Keep calculation snapshots immutable.
-   Do not allow client-side code to set an approved configuration
    version without server validation.
-   Avoid sending detailed internal rates or test scenarios to analytics
    providers.
-   Establish retention and access rules for saved scenarios and logs.

14. TEST STRATEGY
======================================================================

14.1 Unit tests Create deterministic tests for each calculation
function: - Minimum and maximum principal. - Every tenor boundary. -
Rate band boundaries and immediately adjacent values. - Zero, negative,
malformed, and excessive precision inputs. - Missing or expired rates. -
Tax/withholding calculations. - Rounding. - Early settlement and
withdrawal rules once approved. - Installment schedule totals and final
balance. - Product status behavior.

14.2 Golden/reference tests Store approved examples as machine-readable
fixtures. Each fixture must include: - Product code. - Configuration
version. - Inputs. - Expected outputs. - Expected line items. - Expected
status. - Source document and section. - Approval status and date.

Seed examples: - Nano: NGN 20,000, 7 days, 0.30% per day -> interest NGN
420; total NGN 20,420. - Payday: NGN 200,000, 15 days, 0.30% per day ->
interest NGN 9,000; total NGN 209,000. - Private flat: NGN 6,000,000, 6
months, 2% monthly -> interest NGN 720,000; total NGN 6,720,000;
installment NGN 1,120,000 per month under equal-principal convention. -
Private reducing balance: NGN 6,000,000, 6 months, 2% monthly ->
interest NGN 420,000; total NGN 6,420,000 under equal-principal
convention. - RTN maturity: NGN 1,000,000, 90 days, 18.75% p.a. -> gross
interest NGN 46,232.88; WHT NGN 4,623.29; net interest NGN 41,609.59;
payout NGN 1,041,609.59, subject to approved rounding policy.

14.3 Property/invariant tests - Total repayable = principal + total
interest + approved fees/taxes as appropriate. - For reducing balance,
closing balance should not become negative and final principal balance
should be zero within approved rounding tolerance. - Net interest =
gross interest - applicable tax/deductions. - RTN maturity payout =
principal + net interest. - Same input + same config version + same
formula version must produce the same output. - A missing rate must
never return a fabricated projection. - Saved scenario output must match
the original calculation snapshot. - Changing configuration must not
mutate previous results.

14.4 Integration tests - API rejects malformed and unsupported
requests. - API response schema is stable. - Configuration lookup
selects the correct effective rate. - Historical scenarios retain their
configuration version. - Access controls prevent unauthorized config
editing. - No financial transaction endpoint is called by the
calculator.

14.5 End-to-end tests - Select product -> enter valid values ->
calculate -> inspect breakdown. - Invalid amount/tenor shows an
actionable validation message. - Missing rate shows a blocked state. -
Save, reopen, and compare a scenario. - Export a calculation and verify
output values. - Update a draft config and verify it does not affect
active calculations until approved/activated. - Verify mobile and
desktop layout.

Use the existing Rosabon QA Calculation Matrix as the initial execution
checklist, and convert only approved/confirmed expected values into
automated golden tests. Mark unresolved cases as blocked rather than
inventing expected values.

======================================================================
17. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
======================================================================

The MVP is acceptable when: 1. A user can select every product in the
agreed MVP scope. 2. Products with approved calculation rules produce
deterministic estimates and a detailed breakdown. 3. Products without
approved rates/formulas clearly show VALIDATION_ONLY or BLOCKED and do
not fabricate a numeric result. 4. Inputs are validated against the
active product configuration. 5. Every result records the product,
config version, formula version, inputs, outputs, assumptions, and
warnings. 6. Monetary calculations use decimal-safe arithmetic. 7.
Confirmed golden tests pass. 8. Scenario history is reproducible and
immutable. 9. The UI and API show consistent values. 10. No live money
movement, booking, disbursement, or ledger mutation is possible through
the calculator. 11. A tester can export results and compare them with an
observed application result. 12. Known unresolved rules are visible in
documentation and are not silently implemented as assumptions.

======================================================================
19. DEFINITION OF DONE
======================================================================

A product calculator is complete only when: - Product/Finance has
approved its calculation rules and configuration. - Engineering has
implemented the calculation as a testable domain service. - Golden tests
cover the approved examples and key boundaries. - The UI shows inputs,
rate/version, calculation steps, totals, and warnings. - API and UI
results match. - Missing or invalid configuration fails safely. - A
tester can reproduce and export a result. - The calculation is
documented and linked to its source/approval. - No unresolved assumption
is presented as a confirmed product rule.
